01 /ABOUT ME

Research Background

I study how emission sources and atmospheric chemical environments shape secondary aerosol formation, and how this understanding can inform air pollution control.

My work combines high-resolution observations, chemical thermodynamics and kinetics, source apportionment, atmospheric modeling, and interpretable machine learning. I received my Ph.D. in Environmental Science from Nankai University in 2025 and was a CSC-supported visiting doctoral researcher at the University of Birmingham in 2024.

I have published 11 papers as first/corresponding author, including Nature Communications (1), Environmental Science & Technology (2), Geophysical Research Letters (1) and Environmental Science & Technology Letters (1).

03 /RESEARCH DIRECTIONS

Aerosol Processes, Sources and Models

Connecting observations and models to understand where aerosols come from, how they form, and what controls them.

I.

Aerosol Chemistry & Formation

Secondary sulfate and nitrate formation, aqueous oxidation pathways, aerosol acidity, and multiphase acid–base buffering.

II.

Sources & Air Quality

High-resolution source apportionment, PMF/ME2 receptor modeling, source–chemistry interactions, and evidence for emission control.

III.

Modeling & Machine Learning

Chemical transport modeling, thermodynamic calculations, interpretable machine learning, and deep-learning surrogates for air quality.
